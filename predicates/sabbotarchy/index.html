Regime form · coined predicate

Sabbotarchy

Rule by sabotage — the structural condition in which the regime achieves its objectives through sustained dysfunction in public-facing functions, while framing the dysfunction as incompetence.

Coinage · linguistic-autopsy procedure

sabotage − initial phoneme → sabbot-
+ Greek root -archy (rule, regime form)
= Sabbotarchy

What it names

A regime form, not a governance mode. The choice of -archy over the colloquial -cracy is structural: -archy sits in the etymological family of regime types (monarchy, oligarchy, hierarchy, anarchy), while -cracy sits in the family of governance mechanisms. Sabbotarchy is a structural category of rule.

The regime achieves its objectives through failure in public-facing functions. The failure is not byproduct; it is the operating logic. Coverage losses produce wealth-transfer flows. Permit delays produce extraction concessions. Audit incapacity produces revenue shifts. The dysfunction is consistent with structural benefit to known coalitions.

Operating mechanism

Sabbotarchy operates by exploiting a property of public discourse: visible incompetence is more politically defensible than visible sabotage. Critics who treat each "failure" as isolated incompetence accept the protective layer the mode depends on. Critics tracking the pattern across cases render the layer ineffective.

The mode requires a framing-layer to operate. Without obfuscation it would be recognized. Without sabotage it would have nothing to obfuscate. The compound — Obfuscratic Sabbotarchy — captures the operational reality.

Distinction from adjacent predicates

PredicateWhat it asserts about the badness
KakistocracyActors are genuinely incompetent. The badness is a property of the actors.
PathocracyActors are psychologically dysfunctional. The badness is downstream of pathology.
SabbotarchyThe badness is the operational design. Incompetence-framing is the deception layer.
AnemocracyFailures are downstream of absent coherent direction.
IgnorocracyFailures are downstream of absent knowledge.
BureaucratocracyFailures are downstream of procedural ossification.

Five distinct predicates, all of which could be casually described as "they're bad at this." The discrimination matters because the interventions differ. Sabbotarchy requires accountability for deliberate damage; Kakistocracy requires personnel replacement; conflating them produces consistently misallocated intervention.
