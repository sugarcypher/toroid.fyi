About toroid.fyi

A torus is the simplest surface that captures what an edge can't: pressure that loops back, flanking that's constant, feedback that re-enters its own origin.

Premise

Most game boards and most diagrams assume an edge. Edges create corners. Corners create privilege — the player who holds the corner can be flanked from at most two sides instead of four. Edges also lie: in real systems where feedback loops back on itself, there is no corner to hold.

toroid.fyi treats the torus as a research substrate. Bounded (finite play surface). Edgeless (no corners to camp). Doubly periodic (pressure on the north wraps to the south; pressure on the east wraps to the west). What strategies survive when you cannot retreat into an edge?

The nine forms

The site began (May 2026) as three artifacts of one investigation and has grown to nine, arranged on the surface in the order the work is done — observe, name, measure, predict, check, correct:

  1. Polymorphic Catalysis Toroid — observe. Relational instrument: 137 actors, 118 targets, 349 operational cells, 130 actor-actor relationships, 50 wormhole backchannels.
  2. Polymorphic Matrix — name. The precursor (March 2026): the first map of the circulation that turned out to need a torus.
  3. Named and Counted, Not Blamed nor Accounted — name. The companion volume: the units.
  4. The Substrate Attack — name. The condition every instrument instances: cut the load-bearing layer, frame the cut, collect on the damage.
  5. The Removal Economy — observe and measure. One extraction loop traced end to end, with numbers.
  6. Obfuscratic Sabbotarchy — the Deception Arcane — measure. Five-dimensional scoring of government-services-redirection architectures; its instance register locks predictions with check dates.
  7. Sches — predict. Ludic substrate: a playable game that exercises the toroidal commitment loop.
  8. The Two Cycles — correct. The meta-instrument: the regime's extraction cycle against the observer's measurement cycle, with the gaps shown.
  9. The System — the index beneath: papers, terminology, method, archive, legal.

They share a topology because the topology is what they are about: two independent cycles, which is exactly what a torus is.
