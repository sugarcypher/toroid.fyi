What this is

The companion volume to the Deception Arcane — the book-length argument behind the live instrument. The claim is small to say and hard to earn: a kind of governing can be measured, not merely alleged. Five signatures; one composite score; a way to watch how fast that score climbs, and whether the institutions climb together; a set of worked cases; and, by design, a control group of prior reforms the tool should rate low — because a tool that can only rate things high measures nothing at all.

It measures; it does not pronounce. Where it says a thing happened, there is a record. Where it says a thing is projected, there is a model — not a body count. Where it scores, the score is an argument asking to be answered.

What it is not

Proof. The scores are judgments — anchored to dated public evidence, but judgments, and so far mostly one person's. The grimmest numbers — coverage lost, revenue lost, deaths modeled — are forecasts, and they say so every time they appear. The title is the whole posture in five words: named and counted is the author's work; blamed and accounted is the reader's.

The evidence tags

  • Occurred — a documented event verifiable against the contemporaneous public record.
  • Projected — a modeled or forecast outcome; legitimate as evidence but never presented as realized.
  • Contested — magnitude, attribution, or characterization is disputed; confidence turns on the quality of the contestation, not its existence.
  • Correlation — a measured relationship between variables, without implied causal direction.

Relation to the portfolio

The book is the argument; the instrument is the apparatus. Part I builds the concept and the five signatures; Parts II–VI build the measurement machinery and the negative controls; Part VII applies it. The coined vocabulary is defined on the predicates pages; the evidence layer is the Polymorphic Catalysis Toroid.
